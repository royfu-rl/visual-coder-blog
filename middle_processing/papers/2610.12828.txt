 TreeHCA : Hindsight-Guided Exploration and Credit Assignment for Multi-Turn LLM Agents

 Abstract 

 Outcome-supervised group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) gives every token in an LLM agent’s trajectory the same advantage, leaving useful and unhelpful decisions undifferentiated. Hindsight credit assignment (HCA) can refine this feedback, but its estimates depend on trajectories containing informative alternatives at consequential histories. We introduce TreeHCA , which couples sampling via hindsight ratios to generate these alternatives with hindsight credit assignment to evaluate their contribution. We use changes in reference-answer likelihood to revisit intermediate histories and select alternative continuations, forming a tree of reasoning and tool interactions. This exploration changes the distribution of sampled continuations. We derive the corresponding inverse-hindsight importance correction, including an exact finite-pool identity, and use its structure in a normalized tree backup that propagates terminal outcomes into turn-level advantages. This common hindsight structure is TreeHCA ’s central design principle, linking informative exploration to hindsight-guided credit assignment. Across seven question-answering benchmarks and three Qwen backbones, TreeHCA outperforms every baseline in aggregate exact match, validating our paired design for multi-turn LLM training. Our code is available at
 github.com/TheBlackCat22/TreeHCA .

 1 Introduction

 An LLM agent solves a multi-turn task through reasoning steps and tool calls. With terminal verification, success feedback arrives only after a rollout is completed. Outcome-supervised group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) converts this feedback into a trajectory advantage and assigns it to every generated token ( Shao et al., 2024 ) . A successful trajectory can reinforce unhelpful turns, while a failed one can penalize useful decisions. Therefore, a central challenge in providing feedback for training multi-turn LLM agents is to score intermediate decisions by their contribution to success.

 How should we assign credit to intermediate decisions? Hindsight credit assignment (HCA) relates an action’s probability to the subsequent outcome ( Harutyunyan et al., 2019 ) . An action that appears disproportionately often in successful continuations receives a larger success-conditioned hindsight ratio. This ratio has an exact change-of-measure interpretation, which connects outcome-conditioned observations to the action value under the original policy. In a language agent, the space of interaction histories is large, so the usefulness of this construction depends on obtaining informative alternatives from comparable histories. GiGPO forms groups of comparable states to obtain step-level relative advantages, and HCAPO uses outcome-conditioned LLM scores to refine trajectory feedback ( Feng et al., 2025 ; Tan et al., 2026 ) . These methods make intermediate credit available from policy-generated chains. However, their estimates critically depend on these chains containing informative alternatives. In practice, such alternatives may be sparse at consequential histories, leaving the sampled trajectories insufficiently informative for turn-level credit assignment.

 It is therefore crucially important to gather this information by revisiting histories from which alternative decisions can change the outcome. When an observed turn reduces the likelihood of success, we need to return to its parent and sample new continuations while retaining the original path. This produces comparisons under a shared prefix. Entropy-guided exploration allocates sampling effort to uncertain decisions ( Dong et al., 2026 ; Zong et al., 2026 ) . However, uncertainty alone does not reveal a decision’s contribution to success. We instead use hindsight to guide exploration toward successful outcomes. Hindsight scores identify turns worth revisiting and promising alternatives to sample from their parent histories. We therefore introduce hindsight-guided sampling as an exploration strategy for obtaining the information needed to evaluate intermediate decisions.

 We then make a complementary connection that is key to this paper: how we gather evidence through exploration determines how we must weight it when estimating turn-level advantages. By selecting continuations through hindsight, we change their distribution in the rollout tree. We must therefore account for that selection when estimating advantages for the original rollout policy. For a hindsight-tilted proposal, we derive the inverse hindsight ratio as the required importance weight. Our central insight is this correspondence: the hindsight ratio guides exploration toward informative continuations, and its inverse accounts for that sampling preference when estimating credit. 

 We develop this correspondence in TreeHCA . Its core idea has two parts. First, we use hindsight-guided sampling to generate informative trajectories. Changes in the model’s likelihood of the reference answer identify turns to revisit and guide our selection of alternative continuations. Second, we use credit assignment to samples to accord advantages to intermediate turns according to the outcomes of their continuations. We propagate group-normalized terminal rewards through the tree using inverse hindsight weights to counterbalance the selection preference. The resulting turn-level signals reflect the successful and unsuccessful continuations supported by each intermediate decision. The key to TreeHCA is that these operations follow the same hindsight change of measure: the score that directs exploration also determines the inverse correction used to compute credit. This pairing is the central design principle behind the gains evaluated in Section 6 . See Figure 1 .

 We make three contributions. (1) Hindsight-guided exploration. We formulate an exploration rule that uses turn-level hindsight scores to select histories and alternative continuations, connecting the exact hindsight ratio to candidate action values. (2) Hindsight-guided credit assignment. We derive the importance correction for hindsight-guided selection, including an exact finite-pool identity, and use its inverse-score structure to construct turn-level advantages through a normalized tree backup. (3) Experimental validation of the paired construction. We evaluate TreeHCA on seven question-answering benchmarks with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B, and Qwen3-8B. Across the reported model and task settings, TreeHCA outperforms all compared methods in aggregate exact match (EM). The paired design also achieves the strongest EM and F1 in the component study.

 2 Related Work

 Intermediate credit from terminal outcomes.

 HCA expresses action values through outcome-conditioned action probabilities ( Harutyunyan et al., 2019 ) . For LLM agents, GiGPO constructs groups around recurring or comparable environment states and combines step-level and trajectory-level advantages ( Feng et al., 2025 ) . HCAPO uses the LLM as a hindsight critic, normalizes its outcome-conditioned action scores within a trajectory, and combines them with group-relative feedback ( Tan et al., 2026 ) . IGPO uses changes in reference-answer likelihood as information-gain rewards ( Wang et al., 2026 ) . These mechanisms extract intermediate learning signals from linear rollouts. TreeHCA uses outcome relevance to select the continuations supplying these signals.

 Tree sampling and turn-level learning.

 Tree-GRPO shares prefixes in LLM-agent rollouts and constructs relative advantages within and across trees ( Ji et al., 2026 ) . In diffusion models, TreeGRPO backs terminal advantages through a denoising tree ( Ding & Ye, 2026 ) . AEPO allocates sampling using entropy-based information proxies and shapes the optimization signal, while AT 2 PO combines entropy-guided exploration with an entropy-weighted tree backup ( Dong et al., 2026 ; Zong et al., 2026 ) . IGRPO uses reference-answer likelihood to guide adaptive rollout expansion ( Zhang et al., 2026 ) . These approaches motivate examining exploration and credit together. Our focus is the sampling distribution induced by hindsight selection and the corresponding inverse weighting in the backup. The separate policy-update ratio compares the updated and behavior policies. Appendix A provides a detailed review of these sampling and credit mechanisms.

 4 Hindsight-Guided Exploration

 We first develop TreeHCA ’s hindsight exploration policy. This is the sampling part of the paired construction: we use scores indicative of final outcome likelihood to decide where to gather informative alternatives. A turn that reduces the likelihood of success identifies a parent history worth revisiting. We sample from the history before that decision, so an alternative can change both the next action and the evidence subsequently available to the agent. Retaining the original continuation provides a comparison under the same prefix. We formalize this criterion through the exact hindsight distribution, then construct its practical score and tree-sampling rule. The terminal verifier evaluates each retained path, supplying the outcomes whose credit we compute in Section 5 .

 5 Hindsight-Guided Credit Assignment

 We now develop the complementary part of the pair which includes assigning credit to the samples gathered by TreeHCA ’s exploration policy. Hindsight-guided selection favors high-scoring continuations, while the learning signal must reflect their value under the original rollout policy. The same scores that guide exploration therefore determine the inverse weighting needed for estimation. We derive this correspondence for the specified sampling laws and use its inverse-score structure to construct turn-level advantages on the tree.

Inverse weighting compensates for this preference when computing a policy-based expectation. The direct-tilt identity gives the correction for score-proportional sampling, and the finite-pool identity gives it conditional on the sampled candidates. TreeHCA carries the same inverse-score structure into a normalized backup over retained children, propagating their outcome signals to intermediate turns.  Advantages are held fixed during the update. A shared-prefix turn receives the same node advantage in every descendant trajectory, with the sequence ratio evaluated for each complete trajectory.

 6 Experiments

 We now conduct a set of experiments to answer two fundamental questions. Does hindsight-guided exploration collect informative trajectories? Chain-method comparisons place intermediate credit signals in context, and tree-method comparisons examine the choice of exploration rule. Does inverse-hindsight weighting provide useful turn-level credit? The component comparison changes exploration and credit rules in turn, thereby examining their contribution to the complete method.

 7 Conclusion

 TreeHCA connects exploration and intermediate credit through a common hindsight change of measure. Hindsight scores guide the selection of alternative continuations, and inverse weights account for that sampling preference when estimating advantages. We derive this relationship for a hindsight-tilted proposal and finite-pool candidate selection, then realize it through a normalized tree backup. Across the evaluated question-answering settings, the paired construction improves on the compared baselines, and component ablations connect its performance to the combination of exploration and credit. Together, the sampling identities and component experiments identify the pairing as the central design principle behind these gains.

 Appendix A Detailed related work

 Intermediate credit assignment depends on both the information available at a decision and the continuations used to assess it. We organize the relevant methods by how they collect alternatives and translate outcomes into learning signals.

 A.1 Hindsight distributions and practical action credit

 Hindsight credit assignment (HCA) measures an action’s relevance through its probability conditioned on a later state or return ( Harutyunyan et al., 2019 ) . Bayes’ rule relates this posterior-to-policy ratio to the action’s effect on future outcomes. Under exact conditionals and appropriate support, the forward ratio recovers action-conditioned values from policy-mixture outcomes, while the inverse ratio recovers policy-mixture expectations from action-conditioned outcomes. For binary rewards, the success-conditioned ratio ranks actions by expected reward at a common parent. These identities connect outcome relevance to a change of measure.

 GiGPO obtains practical credit by combining episode-relative returns with discounted-return comparisons among actions at recurring anchor states ( Feng et al., 2025 ) . It matches environment-state keys, using sequence similarity for search-QA histories. These comparisons can link actions at different times or histories sharing an anchor. HCAPO uses the actor as a generative hindsight critic ( Tan et al., 2026 ) . It scores recorded actions with successful-outcome context, applies length and temperature normalization, divides each score by its trajectory mean, and clips the multiplier before combining discounted-return and trajectory-relative feedback. Both obtain intermediate signals from collected trajectories. TreeHCA uses the exact hindsight conditional for its ranking interpretation and reference-answer scores to direct the collection of alternatives.

 A.2 Outcome and process feedback along policy trajectories

 Outcome-supervised GRPO assigns each response’s standardized reward to its generated tokens, comparing responses to the same prompt ( Shao et al., 2024 ) . Its process-supervised formulation instead accumulates intermediate reward-model scores. Search-R1 learns interleaved reasoning and retrieval from terminal answer rewards, using PPO with a learned value model by default and GRPO as an alternative ( Jin et al., 2025 ) . Retrieved passages condition subsequent decisions, while losses apply to generated tokens. ZeroSearch trains with a retrieval simulator and a curriculum of increasingly noisy observations, combining terminal answer feedback with experience under changing retrieval conditions ( Sun et al., 2025 ) .

 Process rewards supply information about intermediate search decisions. StepSearch rewards gains in gold-evidence coverage, penalizes repeated retrieval, and adds answer and query supervision ( Zheng et al., 2025b ) . PPO propagates these rewards through value estimates and generalized advantage estimation. IGPO instead uses changes in the reference answer’s length-normalized log likelihood as dense turn rewards, normalizing process and outcome feedback separately ( Wang et al., 2026 ) . These methods refine credit within collected chains. In TreeHCA , answer information also determines which histories and decisions receive additional rollout budget.

 A.3 Tree structure, exploration criteria, and credit aggregation

 Shared-prefix trees compare continuations from a common history while reusing earlier interaction. The LLM-agent Tree-GRPO of Ji et al. (2026) expands randomly selected nonleaf steps and combines reward-normalized advantages within and across trees. Shared-prefix occurrences accumulate contributions from descendant trajectories. The diffusion-model TreeGRPO of Ding & Ye (2026) samples alternative denoising paths within a stochastic window and propagates normalized terminal advantages through child weights proportional to behavior-policy likelihoods. These distinct methods connect tree structure to credit in agent interactions and latent denoising, respectively.

 Entropy provides another exploration criterion. AT 2 PO ranks histories using sampled-token log probabilities and a penalty for repeated parent expansion, then aggregates leaf signals through entropy-weighted backups and applies a turn-level policy objective ( Zong et al., 2026 ) . AEPO uses a pilot trajectory to allocate sampling between the root and intermediate histories according to root and tool-related entropy ( Dong et al., 2026 ) . It spreads exploration through a consecutive-expansion penalty and combines entropy-shaped advantages with a clipping modification that preserves positive-advantage gradients beyond the upper threshold. TreeHCA connects history and candidate selection to reference-answer compatibility and the corresponding sampling correction.

 A.4 The sampling distribution and policy optimization

 IGRPO directly connects answer information to rollout allocation ( Zhang et al., 2026 ) . It combines length-normalized answer likelihood with improvement over the parent and exponentially transforms this score into expansion probabilities. Under a fixed policy, equal horizon, and growing per-layer budgets, its analysis gives an exponentially tilted trajectory distribution with a KL-regularized teacher interpretation. Group-relative training uses samples from this teacher. Our estimation target is the original rollout-policy expectation at a selected parent, connecting continuation selection to the reciprocal weighting that recovers that target.

 For an independently generated candidate pool with positive selection probabilities, Equation 11 gives an exact correction: conditional weighting recovers the pool mean, and averaging over candidate generation recovers the base-policy expectation. The practical tree backup in Equation 14 normalizes inverse proxy scores over retained siblings. Under independent draws from the specified direct tilt, the corresponding self-normalized estimator is consistent ( Section D.4 ). The sampling law therefore supplies the inverse-score form used in credit aggregation.

 Policy optimization addresses a separate distribution change between rollout and updated policies. GSPO uses a length-normalized sequence ratio, while GSPO-token allows token-specific advantages through a stop-gradient construction ( Zheng et al., 2025a ) . In TreeHCA , this optimizer acts on turn-level advantages after tree credit has been computed. The exploration rule and credit operator share a common principle: credit determines which alternatives are collected, and their collection law informs the credit assigned to their outcomes.

 Appendix E Tree-construction implementation details

 Two details clarify the construction in Algorithm 1 . First, the practical hindsight score in Equation 7 uses a completed turn n = ( u , o ) n=(u,o) . We execute every candidate against the environment before scoring it, then continue only the selected candidate to termination. One exploration step therefore costs N N turn executions and a single full rollout. Second, line 3 clips the score at κ br \kappa_{\mathrm{br}} , which assigns exactly the value κ br \kappa_{\mathrm{br}} to every node that is not flagged. Taking the K K smallest scores with uniform tie-breaking therefore expands the flagged nodes in increasing order of score and fills any remaining slots uniformly at random, which is the selection rule described in Section 4 .

 For the Qwen2.5-3B experiments, we use a rollout-matched variant of TreeHCA. Rather than first completing a fixed set of initial trajectories and branching afterward, we monitor the hindsight ratio during rollout generation. Whenever the hindsight ratio falls below a predefined threshold, we branch from that decision point and sample two alternative children. This keeps the total rollout budget comparable to GRPO while concentrating additional exploration on decisions identified as potentially consequential by the hindsight signal.
